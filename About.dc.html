About

I'd rather be useful than first.

I'm AJ. TooGoodTech is 93 videos of me using technology properly and then telling you what I found — including the parts a spec sheet leaves out.

The channel is basically my buying process, filmed.

Most reviews start the same way: I need something, I read too much about it, and I still can't tell whether the cheap one is fine. So I get it, use it for real, and film the answer I wish someone had given me.

That's why the videos run long. A keyboard sounds different after a month. A budget headset gives something up, and it's my job to find out what. A recovery tool either gets your files back or it doesn't. Those answers take time, and they don't fit in a thirty-second clip.

I'm not chasing launch-day coverage or trying to sound like a magazine. I'd rather be the video someone finds three months later, when they're actually about to buy.

How I work

Four rules I don't break.

01
Live with it first

Nothing gets reviewed from a press release. It goes on my desk, in my ears, or on my wrist until I have an opinion I'd defend.

02
Show the real thing

Raw audio for mics and headsets, real typing for keyboards, actual screens for software. If you can hear or see it, I don't describe it — I show it.

03
Name the compromise

Everything at a price has a catch. Saying it out loud is the whole value of the review — and it's why the good verdicts mean something.

04
Sponsored changes nothing

Paid work is disclosed and the verdict stays mine. A brand can review the script; a brand can't rewrite what I thought of the product.

Working with brands

Eight brands have taken that deal so far.

Five paid campaigns — FIFINE, Hume Health, Stellar Data Recovery, NUBWO and Arboleaf — plus product collaborations with Keychron, Neewer and Sabrent. Each one is disclosed in the video, and each one got the same treatment as an unsponsored review.
